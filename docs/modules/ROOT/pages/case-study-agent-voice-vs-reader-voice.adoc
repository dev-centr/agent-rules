This is a worked example from retitling and rewriting the HCI / DevCentr material about broken links and URL identity.
It shows where agent-friendly habits collide with reader-facing voice — even when the house rules already forbid the collision.

== What we were trying to do

Ship a coherent public story for a hard idea: pretty URLs are for people; treating them as the only system identity is why renames leave broken links.

That story needed:

* titles strangers would search and feel
* filenames and wiki slugs that match those titles
* openings that talk like a teacher, not like a handoff memo between agents

== Failure 1: abstract titles before the hook

Early public titles leaned on in-group frames:

* *Docs path renames as unpaid chores* (metaphor before the ache)
* *Path renames that break dependents* (*dependents* is abstract)
* *Labels versus wires* / *Navigating by content* (concept nicknames as the cold open)

People do not walk around thinking in *dependents* or *wires*.
They think *URL*, *broken link*, *rename*.

=== What worked instead

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Face | Public title

| Symptom
| *Broken links after a URL rename*

| Diagnosis / docs H1
| *An alternative to URLs*
|===

*Labels versus wires* stayed useful — as a nickname **after** the hook, not as the billboard.

== Failure 2: “fixing” an implication-dense title

An agent read *An alternative to URLs* as an overclaim (“URLs are not obsolete”) and tried to narrow it to fully scoped thesis titles (*URLs as labels, not wires*).

That is the wrong repair.

*An alternative to URLs* packs a second title the reader invents: *Why would URLs need an alternative?* / *Alternative for what job?*
The body scopes the use-case.
The title’s job is to reopen a habit the reader has already accepted (URL = the string I typed).

House name for that move: **enthymemic / implication-dense** titles.
See xref:editorial-titles.adoc[Editorial titles].

== Failure 3: Cool-URI absorb when nobody depends on the old path

The wiki page kept the slug `navigating-by-content` “for Cool URI absorb” while the public title was already *An alternative to URLs*.

That habit protects real inbound links.
It also freezes opaque names when the audience is still only you and the agents.

Here nobody was relying on the old wiki path.
The slug moved to `an-alternative-to-urls` so title, filename, and URL matched.

Rule of thumb::
Match slug to public title by default.
Keep an absorb redirect only when you can name who still hits the old URL.

== Failure 4: structural chat residue in the reader’s opening

The docs topic briefly opened like an agent briefing:

* “People already chase the *thing*.” (in-group poetry; not a hook)
* “Slug stays… internal nickname… do not confuse with…”
* “Umbrella (systems altitude): … This page remains the HCI symptom ↔ diagnosis/treatment face.”

None of that helps a stranger.
Slug notes, nickname maps, and collision warnings belong in `AGENTS.md` or a changelog.
The published opening owes the reader a conversation.

=== What the opening became

[quote]
____
URLs are a problem.
But why are they a problem, you ask?

Because we ask one string to do two jobs…
____

And the umbrella, in human voice:

[quote]
____
This article sits under the umbrella of Internet Reliability @ DevCentr…
Here we stay on the HCI / DevX face…
____

== Why this keeps happening (even with good rules)

The Audience / POV gate already said: published pages are for the reader on the page, not for the chat commissioner.

Three pressures still win unless you check the gate **after** drafting:

. **Session context outcompetes the stranger.** The chat is full of dialectic maps, Cool URI, nickname collisions, AGENTS maps. Models try to “persist” that on the page so the next agent does not get lost — in the wrong channel.
. **Structure tools become voice.** Symptom ↔ diagnosis tables, umbrella cross-links, and Diátaxis faces are good. Applied badly, they produce handoff prose (“this page remains the Y face”) instead of teaching.
. **Title doctrine ≠ body doctrine.** Fixing titles does not automatically fix ledes. Cold-reader titles can sit above a briefing-memo introduction.

You are not mainly fighting a hidden Cursor system prompt.
You are fighting channel mix: agent-recall constraints leaking into visitor copy.

== Channel split (the durable fix)

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Channel | What belongs there

| Published docs / blog / news
| Concrete hooks, implication-dense titles when earned, casual explanation, umbrella placement for humans

| `AGENTS.md`, skills, changelogs
| Slug history, nicknames, “do not confuse with…”, Cool-URI absorb decisions, session maps
|===

Pass checks that grew out of this case (also in `general/documentation.md`):

* Does the opening use **their** words (URL, broken link, rename)?
* Are slug / nickname / “do not confuse” notes **absent** from the body?
* Could a smart stranger follow the page with this chat closed?
